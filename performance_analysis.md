## 2. Self-Generated Questions (M4 and M5): Business Justifications, Logic Diagrams & Results

### Question M4: Time-Aligned Wet-Hour vs. Dry-Hour Carpark Occupancy Surge (`m4_weather_parking_join.py`)

* **Analytical Question:** Across the 720 clock hours of September 2026 (`2026-09-01 00:00` to `2026-09-30 23:00`), how does Lot Type `C` parking occupancy and high-pressure incidence (`occupancy >= 90%`) shift during **Wet Hours** (hours where `>= 10%` of NEA weather stations record `>= 0.2 mm` rainfall or mean station rainfall is `>= 0.5 mm`) compared to **Dry Hours**, and which top 15 HDB carparks exhibit the largest positive occupancy surge during rain?
* **Business Justification (Transport / Parking Operator):** HDB and urban mobility operators managing commercial hubs, hawker centres, and town-centre carparks face sudden parking congestion during heavy tropical rainstorms when commuters shift from walking, cycling, or open-air transit transfers to private cars or shelter in covered carparks. A single-pass filter or simple column sum cannot answer this question because it requires a **distributed reduce-side join** aligning 5-minute NEA station rainfall readings with hourly HDB carpark snapshots by Singapore clock hour (`YYYY-MM-DD HH:00`), followed by a second aggregation pass per carpark to contrast conditional wet-hour vs. dry-hour occupancy distributions. Identifying the top rain-sensitive carparks enables dynamic rainy-day lot reservation, electronic parking guidance sign alerts, and grace-period adjustments.
* **MapReduce Logic Diagram (3-Step Pipeline — YARN Apps `_0007`, `_0008`, `_0009`):**
  * **Step 1 Mapper (`mapper_hourly_align`):** Reads both `rainfall_clean.csv` and `carpark_clean.csv` from HDFS, normalises timestamps to `Asia/Singapore` (`UTC+08:00`), filters carpark records to valid Lot Type `C` (`total_lots > 0`, `0 <= lots_available <= total_lots`), and emits key `hour_key = "YYYY-MM-DD HH:00"` with tagged values `["RAIN", station_id, "HH:MM", rainfall_mm]` or `["PARK", carpark_id, obs_ts, lots_available, total_lots]`.
  * **Step 1 Shuffle & Reducer (`reducer_hourly_join` — Reduce-Side Join):** Groups all station rainfall slots and carpark snapshots within the same clock hour `YYYY-MM-DD HH:00`. Deduplicates 5-minute rainfall slots per station, computes each station's hourly rainfall (without summing across stations into an islandwide depth), classifies the hour as `"WET"` (`59` wet hours in Sep 2026) or `"DRY"` (`661` dry hours), and emits `carpark_id -> [weather_state, occupancy_ratio, is_high_pressure, mean_station_rain]`.
  * **Step 2 Reducer (`reducer_carpark_comparison`):** Groups by `carpark_id` across all 720 hours (`2,005` evaluated carparks), computes `wet_mean_occupancy`, `dry_mean_occupancy`, `wet_minus_dry_occupancy_delta`, and high-pressure fractions (`>= 90%`), and forwards each carpark profile keyed by `"RANK_ALL"`.
  * **Step 3 Reducer (`reducer_rank_top_sensitive`):** Computes the global summary (`2,005` carparks evaluated) and ranks the **Top 15 Carparks by Wet-Hour Occupancy Surge** (`BM29` ranks #1 with `+33.66%` occupancy delta: `84.60%` wet vs. `50.94%` dry; `HG97` ranks #2 with `+32.44%` delta: `90.62%` wet vs. `58.17%` dry; `HLM` ranks #3 with `+31.46%` delta).

### Question M5: Bidirectional Bus Corridor AM vs. PM Peak Tidal Asymmetry (`m5_bus_directional_asymmetry.py`)

* **Analytical Question:** For each month (`2026-08`, `2026-09`, `2026-10`) and day type (`WEEKDAY`, `WEEKENDS/HOLIDAY`), which bidirectional bus stop pairs `{Stop A, Stop B}` (with combined peak volume `>= 2,000` trips) exhibit the strongest **AM Peak (`06:00`–`09:59`) vs. PM Peak (`17:00`–`20:59`) directional asymmetry and tidal commuter flow**?
* **Business Justification (Bus Fleet & Scheduling Operator):** Public bus operators (SBS Transit, SMRT, Tower Transit, Go-Ahead Singapore) and LTA bus planners scheduling short-working trips and deadhead (empty) bus repositioning need to know which corridors experience extreme **directional imbalance** between morning and evening peaks. A simple sum or count of a single column cannot detect bidirectional asymmetry because forward trips (`Stop A -> Stop B`) and reverse trips (`Stop B -> Stop A`) live in separate rows with swapped origin and destination columns across different hours. Pairing opposite directions under a canonical undirected key `(min(origin, dest), max(origin, dest))` and joining `bus_stops.csv` twice allows planners to pinpoint feeder corridors (such as Sengkang/Punggol LRT-bus interchanges, Tengah -> Beauty World MRT, and Pioneer MRT -> NTU) where buses run packed in one direction and empty in the other.
* **MapReduce Logic Diagram (2-Step Pipeline — YARN Apps `_0010`, `_0011`):**
  * **Step 1 Mapper & Combiner (`mapper_peak_pairs` -> `combiner_peak_pairs`):** Filters `bus_od_clean.csv` (`17,919,803` rows) to AM Peak (`hour in {6,7,8,9}`) and PM Peak (`hour in {17,18,19,20}`), canonicalises each directed pair into undirected order `stop_a = min(origin, dest)` and `stop_b = max(origin, dest)`, maps trip volume into a 4-tuple `[am_a_to_b, am_b_to_a, pm_a_to_b, pm_b_to_a]`, and pre-sums the 4-tuple in the combiner.
  * **Step 1 Reducer (`reducer_tidal_metrics`):** Sums the 4 directional peak buckets per `(year_month, day_type, stop_a, stop_b)`, filters to pairs with `combined_peak >= 2,000` trips and non-zero AM and PM flows, computes `am_a_to_b_share`, `pm_b_to_a_share`, and `tidal_reversal_index = |(am_fwd - am_rev) - (pm_fwd - pm_rev)| / combined_peak`, and re-keys by `(year_month, day_type)`.
  * **Step 2 Reducer (`reducer_init_stops` -> `reducer_rank_tidal`):** Loads `bus_stops_clean.csv` into memory to join descriptions and road names for both `stop_a` and `stop_b`, and ranks the **Top 10 Tidal Corridors** per `(year_month, day_type)` across August, September, and October 2026 (highlighting commuter corridors such as `43009 Bt Batok Int <-> 43719 MINDEF` where AM flow is `99.3%` A->B (`1,816` vs `13`) and PM flow reverses to `99.1%` B->A (`924` vs `8`), and Tengah (`40389 Tengah CC`) <-> `42151 Beauty World Stn`).
